# Luv's Fright Night

A Game Boy Advance platformer in the shape of Super Mario Land 1. Luv is a ghost
with devil horns, an angel halo and a devil tail; the demons all wear halos they
stole; the bosses are the seven deadly sins, and then Hades.

Every sprite, tile, note and sound effect is generated by the scripts in
`tools/` — nothing is sampled or borrowed.

## Building

Needs Docker (OrbStack is fine) and nothing else — the ARM toolchain runs in a
container, so nothing is installed on the Mac.

```bash
./build.sh
```

That produces `LuvsFrightNight.gba`. Open it in mGBA or any GBA emulator.

`./build.sh clean` removes the build directory.

## Checking it still works

```bash
python3 tools/check_levels.py   # every gap crossable from the physics constants
python3 tools/balance.py        # the difficulty curve, world by world
python3 tools/check_audio.py    # peak, RMS, DC offset and clipping
python3 tools/regress.py        # every screen against a blessed baseline
```

`regress.py` builds a clean ROM, replays scripted sessions through the
emulator and compares each frame to `tools/emu/golden/`. It writes a
side-by-side sheet of anything that changed and exits non-zero. When a change
was intended, `python3 tools/regress.py --bless` accepts it as the new truth.

## Regenerating assets

```bash
python3 tools/gen_sprites.py       # graphics/*.bmp + *.json, preview sheets
python3 tools/gen_music.py         # audio/*.xm + rendered previews
python3 tools/gen_sfx.py           # audio/*.wav
python3 tools/gen_preview_page.py  # preview/assets.html (the review page)
python3 tools/gen_manual.py        # scrape/manual.html (the instruction booklet)
python3 tools/gen_scrape.py        # the whole scrape package, manual included
```

## Playing it

The title screen offers Continue, New Game, 2 Player, Level Code, Stage Select
and Extras. Extras holds the high score board, Boss Rush, a sound test, the
credits and a controls screen. Leave the pad alone for twenty seconds and the
cartridge starts an attract loop - the board, then a stage playing itself.

- **2 Player** seats two players on one pad, alternating turns on death, arcade
  style. Each seat keeps its own lives, score and place in the game.
- **Level Code** takes the four-letter code printed on every world card, so
  progress survives a dead battery. World 1-1 is `FJTK`.
- **Continues** — three per run, offered on a countdown when the lives run out.
  The score stands; a continue spends a limited resource, not the run.
- **Best times** are recorded per stage and shown as a column on Stage Select.
  They survive New Game: they are records rather than progress.
- **Cheat Code** under Extras listens for ten seconds for something you may
  already know. The codes are in `dev secrets.md`.
- **Souls** are worth one each; the larger gold ones are worth ten, and there
  are nine of those in every stage. Ninety-nine souls is a life.
- **Three save files** share the cartridge. New Game asks which one; Continue
  only asks when more than one is in use. The score boards and best times sit
  outside the files and survive erasing one.
- **Boss Rush** keeps its own score board, because a five minute rush should
  not have to beat a forty minute run.

The high score board lives on the cartridge and survives a new game too.

| Button | Does |
|---|---|
| D-pad | Run |
| A | Jump. Hold past the apex to hover while the meter lasts. |
| B | Tap: throw a soul flame. Hold: Devil Dash. |

One button covers both, the way B works in Super Mario Land: a tap fires, a hold
runs. Both need their power-up; stomping always works without one.

A hit costs the Purple Soul first, then the Soul Flame, then a life. Fifty
souls is an extra life.

| Power-up | Does |
|---|---|
| Purple Soul | Absorbs one hit. Luv turns violet with a lime outline, so you can see you have it. |
| Soul Flame | Tap B to throw a bouncing flame. |
| Devil Dash | Hold B to run horns-first. |
| Wisp Wings | Triples the hover meter. |

Most power-ups are sealed inside a breakable block: hit it from below and it
drops out onto the floor.

Luv jumps four and a half metatiles, which clears an overhead block row with
room to land on top of it and hover there.

### While you play

START pauses at any point - the clock stops with it - and offers Resume or Quit
to Menu. Clearing a stage banners STAGE CLEAR and the time bonus; beating a sin
banners THE SIN IS UNDONE; losing a life says so. Running out of lives gets a
GAME OVER screen with your score against the cartridge best.

### Cards and the ending

Every stage opens on a card in that world's own masonry: the world numeral, the
sin in Latin and in English, the stage name, and the lives you are carrying in.
It holds for about three seconds, or until you press something.

Beat Hades and the ending runs - Luv drifting upward through a column of souls
while the panels come in, then your score against the best on the cartridge.

### Bosses

Every world ends in an arena: one screen wide, a floor, two ledges, and one of
the seven sins. Stomp it or burn it with a soul flame; touching it any other way
costs you.

| Sin | Moves | Attacks |
|---|---|---|
| Superbia | Holds station on the far side, mirroring you | Shards fall where you are standing |
| Avaritia | Heavy bouncing hops | Lobs its hoard in arcs |
| Luxuria | Floats a figure of eight | Charms demons to fight for it |
| Invidia | Charges wall to wall | Fans shots along the ground |
| Gula | Slow, heavy hops | Wide spread of spit |
| Ira | Charges, and reels when it hits a wall | Shockwaves along the floor |
| Acedia | Never moves | Drops the ceiling on you |
| Hades | Drifts, and speeds up as it loses phases | Changes attack every phase |

Every boss commits to a run at you around its attack. That is the opening: a
boss that only kept its distance would be one you could never reach.

### Score

| For | Points |
|---|---|
| Soul | 10 |
| Breaking a block | 20 |
| Checkpoint | 200 |
| Power-up | 500 |
| 1-Up | 1000 |
| Stomp | 100, doubling for each stomp chained in the air, up to 800 |
| Hitting a boss | 300 |

## The difficulty curve

```bash
python3 tools/balance.py
```

Reports each stage's length, enemies, holes, hazards and clock headroom, then
the average pressure per world. Pressure is enemies per screen, plus holes per
screen weighted higher, plus hazard *regions* - a six-tile lava pit is one
hazard you jump, not six.

It currently climbs 0.80 to 2.09 across the eight worlds with no world easier
than the one before it, and every stage has at least 2.8x the clock it needs.

Two things keep it that way. `difficulty(index)` in `tools/gen_levels.py` runs 0
to 1 across the sixteen stages and every beat scales off it, so which shape a
stage uses no longer decides how hard it is. And each shape carries a weight in
`SHAPES` measured from the generated stages, so the naturally calmer ones gain
more content later in the game.

Stages are built from six shapes - open ground, ledges, pillared halls,
crossings, low cellars and climbs - dealt out by `SHAPE_ORDER` in
`tools/gen_levels.py` so the two halves of a world never play the same way.
| `tools/` | The generators; `tools/emu/` builds a headless mGBA for CI-style checks |
| `preview/` | Human review material, including `assets.html` |

## How the engine fits together

| File | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `src/main.cpp` | Title screen, then the stage loop |
| `src/lfn_game.cpp` | One stage: camera, events, lives, death and clear flow |
| `src/lfn_luv.cpp` | Physics, hover, power-up state, animation |
| `src/lfn_level.cpp` | Tilemap upload, metatile collision, breakable blocks |
| `src/lfn_entities.cpp` | Pooled enemies, pickups and projectiles |
| `src/lfn_boss.cpp` | The eight fights: five ways of moving, five attacks |
| `src/lfn_cards.cpp` | The world card and the ending sequence |
| `src/lfn_backdrop.cpp` | Full-screen room built from any world's tileset |
| `src/lfn_hud.cpp`, `src/lfn_audio.cpp`, `src/lfn_save.cpp` | Status bar, sound, SRAM |
| `include/lfn_tune.h` | Every number that decides how the game feels |

Three things are worth knowing before changing any of it:

- **A level is 16 metatiles tall and its bg map is placed by its centre.** Butano
  positions a background on its middle, so the level background is created at
  `(width/2, height/2)` to make world coordinates start at the top-left corner.
- **A metatile owns four tiles.** The tilesets are 16px wide, so grit emits their
  8x8 tiles as TL, TR, BL, BR - metatile `m` is tile indices `m*4 .. m*4+3`.
  `bn::bg_tiles::set_allow_offset(false)` around background creation stops Butano
  renumbering them.
- **The GBA has 32KB of IWRAM and a small stack.** The map cell buffer and the
  live tile copy are marked `BN_DATA_EWRAM_BSS`, and the stage object is heap
  allocated, because either one on the stack overflows it.

## How the art is made

`tools/pixel.py` is a small indexed-pixel library: shapes, plus automatic
outline, shading and rim-light passes. Because every sprite goes through the
same finishing passes, the whole cast stays on-model. `tools/palette.py` holds
the 16 colours, snapped to the GBA's 5-bit-per-channel space, so previews on the
Mac match the hardware exactly.

Sprites are written as 8bpp BMPs with a 16-colour palette, which grit compresses
to 4bpp in the ROM. Tilesets are 16px wide, so grit emits their 8x8 tiles in the
order TL, TR, BL, BR — metatile `m` therefore owns tile indices `m*4 .. m*4+3`.

## How the music is made

`tools/instruments.py` synthesises every instrument at 32 samples per cycle, so
at XM's 8363 Hz reference rate one cycle is exactly C-4: loops are click-free
and the orchestra is in tune by construction. `tools/xm.py` writes Extended
Module files that Maxmod reads directly, and renders the same song structure to
WAV so a track can be judged before it is in a ROM.

Music is authored quiet and the engine caps it at 0.35 — it is meant to sit
under the action as ambience, not to perform at you.

## Checking a build without an emulator

`tools/emu/` builds a headless mGBA plus `lfn-run`, a harness that boots a ROM,
injects button presses from a script, dumps frames as PPM, and prints the game's
own `LFN_TRACE` output with a frame number attached.

```bash
docker build -t lfn-mgba -f tools/emu/Dockerfile tools/emu   # once
./test.sh
```

`test.sh` builds a traced, invulnerable ROM, autoplays it, prints every gameplay
event it sees, then rebuilds the shipping ROM. `tools/shots.py` turns the
captured frames into a contact sheet.

Build with `-DLFN_TEST_AUTOPILOT=1` and Luv drives himself: he runs right and
jumps when the floor ahead runs out, reading the same collision data the game
does. He is a smoke test, not a good player - he cannot modulate a jump, so a
stall in the autopilot is not by itself evidence that a stage is unfair.

For that there is the linter:

```bash
python3 tools/check_levels.py
```

It reads the physics constants straight out of `include/lfn_tune.h`, then checks
that every gap in every stage can be crossed by a plain jump launched a whole
tile before the edge, with no hovering. Hover should make a crossing
comfortable, never be the only way over it. Gaps too wide for that get stepping
stones from the level generator until they pass.

Scripts live in `tools/emu/*.txt` and understand `wait`, `hold`, `release`,
`tap`, `shot` and `reset`. To aim a screenshot at a particular moment, run once,
read the frame numbers in the trace, and put the `shot` there.

## Getting the engine

`butano/` is a clone of the upstream engine, pinned to the commit this game was
built against. It is deliberately not tracked here - it is somebody else's
repository, and 119 MB of it. To restore it in a fresh checkout:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/GValiente/butano.git butano
git -C butano checkout 77dcbcb3d8783596a9f333c64eedbccec77b05dc
```

Then `./build.sh` as usual. Nothing else needs installing: the toolchain lives
in the `devkitpro/devkitarm` container.
